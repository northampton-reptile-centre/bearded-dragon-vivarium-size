# Bearded Dragon Vivarium Size

Choosing the right **bearded dragon vivarium size** is one of the most important decisions for a UK keeper. Inland bearded dragons (*Pogona vitticeps*) need a long wooden enclosure so they can move between a hot basking area and a cooler end. This guide from [Northampton Reptile Centre](https://www.reptilecentre.com/) explains the adult size to aim for, why hatchling boxes are only temporary, and how to match heating and UVB to that footprint.

## 1. Bearded dragon vivarium size at a glance

| Stage | Recommended enclosure | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Adult (target set-up) | As close to **4 × 2 × 2 ft** (width × depth × height) as possible | Enough length for a proper heat and UV gradient |
| Growing juvenile | Same adult footprint if you can; avoid tiny “starter” boxes long-term | Saves upgrading later and keeps temperatures stable |
| Style | **Wooden vivarium** with large vents and sliding glass doors | Holds heat better than glass tanks in UK rooms |
| Basking zone | About **one-third** of the length under a strong basking lamp | Day basking around 43 °C (100–110 °F) |
| Cool end | Opposite third of the vivarium | Around 27 °C (80 °F) by day so the dragon can cool down |

These targets match our in-store husbandry and our [bearded dragon care sheet](https://www.reptilecentre.com/pages/info-bearded-dragon-care-sheet). Adult *Pogona vitticeps* can reach around 60 cm, so floor length matters more than a tall climbing cage.

## 2. Why bearded dragon vivarium size matters

Bearded dragons come from warm Australian scrub and rocky outcrops. In a UK home they cannot warm up without a clear hot–cool gradient. A short enclosure forces the basking lamp and cool end too close together, so the whole box stays too hot or too cool. A near **4 × 2 × 2 ft** wooden vivarium gives:

- Space to bask, dig, hide and walk
- Room for a ceramic night heater without cooking the cool end
- A usable UVB gradient from back to front when a T5 tube is mounted correctly

Small glass tanks lose heat quickly and rarely give an adult dragon enough length. Use them only as short-term quarantine or rearing housing if you must, then move into a proper wooden set-up.

## 3. Recommended adult size (4 × 2 × 2 ft)

For day-to-day keeping we recommend an enclosure **as close to 4 × 2 × 2 ft as possible**:

- **Width (length along the wall):** about 4 ft — the main axis for the heat gradient
- **Depth:** about 2 ft — room for décor, a UV gradient and a water bowl on the cool side
- **Height:** about 2 ft — suits a ceiling-mounted basking lamp and a Zone 3 T5 UVB tube at a sensible distance from the basking spot

## 4. Heating and lighting inside that footprint

Size alone is not enough — the equipment must suit the box:

1. **Day basking lamp** on one side of the ceiling, on a **dimming thermostat**, aiming for about 43 °C over roughly one-third of the vivarium for 10–12 hours.
2. **Cool end** around 27 °C so the dragon can thermoregulate.
3. **Night warmth** from a guarded **ceramic heater** (no light) on a thermostat — aim for about 27 °C on the warm third while the rest can drop toward room temperature. Lights off at night for a clear day–night cycle.
4. **UVB:** for a 2 ft-tall vivarium we favour a **T5** Zone 3 tube (about 10–12% UVB), mounted so the basking zone sits in the correct UV range. Replace tubes on schedule.

## 5. Décor, substrate and working space

Inside a full-size vivarium, leave climbing and basking décor under the lamp and full/partial hides along the length. Keep food and water bowls on the **cool** side so they do not raise humidity at the hot end. Low-dust, low-humidity substrates (for example coarse beech chip in many store set-ups) suit most arid bearded dragon homes; bioactive soil mixes are an option if you maintain them properly. See [vivarium ornaments](https://www.reptilecentre.com/collections/vivarium-ornaments) and [substrates](https://www.reptilecentre.com/collections/substrates).

Young dragons may need extra cover at first; you can thin décor as they grow so they still have open basking space.

## 7. FAQ

**Is a 3 ft vivarium enough for an adult bearded dragon?**  
A 3 ft box is better than a tiny tank but still short for a full gradient. Aim as close to **4 × 2 × 2 ft** as your space and budget allow.

**Can I start in a small tank and upgrade later?**  
You can house a hatchling briefly in a smaller secure set-up, but plan the adult vivarium from day one. Upgrading late often means redoing heat and UVB.

**Wooden vivarium or glass terrarium?**  
For UK rooms we prefer a **wooden vivarium** with good ventilation. It holds heat more steadily for this desert species.

**Do I need UVB if the vivarium is large?**  
Yes. Size does not replace UVB. Pair the enclosure with a suitable T5 tube and correct mounting height.
